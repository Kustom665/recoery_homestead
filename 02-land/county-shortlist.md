# County Shortlist

## The scoring model

The winner is not the cheapest land. It is the **intersection** of four things.
Score every candidate on all four before you fall in love with a listing.

| Axis | Why it matters | What good looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cheap land** | Land you overpay for is capital you can't put into buildings | Well under the PA median of ~$16,500/acre |
| **Unzoned townships** | No ordinance means no variance, no hearing, no neighbor petition | County where a large share of townships have never adopted zoning |
| **Overdose burden** | Drives the county's opioid settlement allocation *and* gives your grant narrative its evidence base | Above the state average overdose death rate |
| **USDA rural eligibility** | Unlocks USDA Community Facilities loans, the best financing in the stack | Municipality ≤20,000 population |

The third axis is the counterintuitive one. **You want to build where the
problem is worst.** That is where the money is, where the referrals are, and
where a county commissioner will actually return your call.

## The zoning data, because it drives everything

- **822 Pennsylvania municipalities have no zoning ordinance at all.**
- Rural counties: only **34%** of municipalities have zoning. Urban counties: 82%.
- Northwest PA: **30%** zoned. Central PA: **39%** zoned.
- Southeast PA: **99%** zoned. Southcentral: 81%.
- Of the 12 southwestern counties (Allegheny, Beaver, Butler, Cambria, Fayette,
  Greene, Indiana, Lawrence, Mercer, Somerset, Washington, Westmoreland), only
  **three** have countywide zoning: Greene, Fayette, Indiana.
- **Nearly 35% of all land area** in that 12-county southwestern region has no
  zoning of any kind.
- **Westmoreland County** has no county zoning ordinance.
- **Susquehanna County**: only **6 of 40** municipalities have adopted zoning.

### The county-backstop trap — check this before anything else

"This township has no zoning ordinance" does **not** mean the land is unzoned.
A county can adopt a countywide ordinance that automatically fills in wherever a
municipality has none. Two of the counties people reach for first are exactly
this case:

- **Schuylkill County** adopted a countywide zoning ordinance on 22 Dec 2010,
  effective 27 Dec 2010, which by its own terms "applies throughout geographic
  areas of Schuylkill County where a City, Township or Borough does not have a
  municipal zoning ordinance in effect." **There is no unzoned land in
  Schuylkill County.** Every parcel is zoned by someone.
- **Fayette County** administers zoning for **31 of its 42 municipalities**; the
  remaining 11 run their own ordinances. Effectively **fully zoned**, just split
  between two administrators.

So the question is never "does this township have zoning?" It is **"does this
township have zoning, *and* does the county have a backstop ordinance?"** Ask
both, in writing, of the county planning office. This one question is the
difference between a by-right build and a nine-month hearing calendar.

## Candidates

### Tier 1 — best overall fit

**Susquehanna County (Northeast)** — ✅ **verified, and the only Tier 1 pick
that survives the county-backstop test**

Probably the single best fit in the state.

- Only 6 of 40 municipalities have zoning — roughly **85% of the county is
  unzoned**
- **No countywide zoning backstop.** The county has adopted a Subdivision and
  Land Development Ordinance (SALDO) **only**. Its own Planning Commission
  states that because the county has a SALDO and not a zoning ordinance, the
  Commission *"cannot, under its Ordinance, tell someone they cannot propose a
  specific type of development on a specific piece of land."* That sentence is
  the whole reason this county is at the top of the list.
- The Planning Commission is **advisory only** in municipal matters
- Culturally hostile to zoning — the county planning director has described
  residents as "very proud of owning their land," and zoning proposals have
  repeatedly gone nowhere
- Named among the counties with the **highest overdose death rates** in PA
- Overwhelmingly rural, so USDA Community Facilities eligibility is easy

**Real land pricing (the reason this is affordable):**

| Measure | Susquehanna | Lancaster |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Median price/acre, **actual sales** (325 records) | **~$9,300** | — |
| Median price/acre, current listings | ~$13,300 | — |
| Undeveloped land, median/acre | ~$10,000 | — |
| Farmland/acre | — | **~$22,000** |

Treat per-acre medians with care: they are computed across all parcel sizes, and
small lots carry far higher per-acre prices than large tracts. A 30–40 acre
wooded parcel should come in **below** the county median per acre, not at it.
Budget from the sold-price column, never the asking-price column.

- Downside: far from your Bucks/Montgomery/Berks base — roughly a 2.5–3 hour
  drive. That matters for your own involvement and for staff recruitment.
  **This is the real cost of the unzoned advantage, and it is not small.**

**Fayette County (Southwest)**

- **Highest overdose death rate in Pennsylvania (~35 per 100,000)** — the
  strongest possible grant narrative and a large settlement allocation
- Cheap southwestern PA land
- Rural, USDA-eligible
- Laurel Highlands / Ohiopyle country — genuinely beautiful, genuinely farmable
- Downside: **effectively fully zoned.** The county planning office administers
  zoning for **31 of the 42 municipalities**; the other 11 run their own
  ordinances. There is no unzoned corner to slip into here.
- The mitigating point still stands: county zoning is often *easier* than 30
  separate township ordinances — you deal with professional planning staff once,
  not a volunteer board with a grudge. But plan on a zoning process, not a
  by-right build.

**Westmoreland County (Southwest)**

- **No county zoning ordinance**
- Overdose death rate ~30 per 100,000
- Reasonable land prices, large rural townships
- Closer to Pittsburgh, so more services, staff, and hospital partners nearby

### Tier 2 — strong, worth pulling listings

- **Cambria County** — cheap, rural, historically heavy overdose burden.
  Fatal overdoses fell sharply in 2024 (24 deaths, a 21-year low, down from 62
  in 2023), which is good news for the county and slightly weakens the "acute
  crisis" framing in a grant application. Still a strong build site.
- **Greene County** — very cheap, very rural, countywide zoning.
- **Somerset County** — cheap, rural, largely unzoned townships.
- **Venango, Clearfield, Elk** — northwest/central, cheapest tier in the state,
  30–39% zoned. Elk was named among higher overdose-rate counties.
- **Clinton, Columbia, Northumberland, Bradford, Potter** — central and northern
  tier, cheap, mostly unzoned.

### Tier 3 — closest to home, but you pay for it

- **Schuylkill County** — closest of the genuinely affordable counties to your
  home base, roughly 1 to 1.5 hours from Berks. **If driving distance is a hard
  constraint, start here** — but go in with both eyes open:
  - **Countywide zoning backstop since 27 Dec 2010.** No unzoned land exists in
    this county. You *will* run a zoning process. The FHA
    reasonable-accommodation strategy in `03-zoning/` is your primary tool here,
    not a fallback.
  - Recent rural land sales show a **median of $16,667/acre**, but that figure
    is on small parcels averaging ~12.3 acres. Per-acre pricing drops
    substantially on larger tracts, so do not read that median as the price of a
    40-acre farm.
  - Net: you trade the zoning advantage for ~90 minutes of drive time. That may
    well be the right trade — proximity keeps a founder engaged and makes staff
    recruitment possible — but it is a **trade**, not a free lunch.
- **Luzerne County** — overdose rate ~25 per 100,000, moderate land costs,
  mixed zoning coverage.
- **Berks County** — your home turf, real opioid settlement program through the
  Council on Chemical Abuse, but southeastern PA land prices and near-universal
  zoning.

### Excluded

- **Lancaster** — see `lancaster-reality-check.md`. One pass, four fails.
- **Bucks, Montgomery** — most expensive land in the state, 99% zoned.
  Montgomery has a large opioid settlement pot ($59.7M) which is genuinely
  attractive, but you cannot afford to build there.

## The recommendation

The choice collapses to a single question: **is the unzoned advantage worth
relocating for?**

**Susquehanna County is the only county on this list where you can build
by right.** No countywide backstop, ~85% of municipalities unzoned, a planning
commission that says in writing it cannot prohibit a proposed use, sold-price
median around $9,300/acre, and a real overdose burden to anchor the grant
narrative. Nothing else in the state combines all five. The price is a 2.5–3
hour drive from your base.

**Everything else on this list means running a zoning process.** Schuylkill,
Fayette, Greene, and Indiana all have county backstops. That is not fatal —
the FHA reasonable-accommodation route in `03-zoning/` is a genuine legal
pathway with real teeth, and plenty of recovery housing gets built through it.
But it adds months, legal fees, and a public hearing where neighbors show up.

**How to decide, honestly:** if you will realistically be on site several times
a week for the first two years, distance beats zoning — take Schuylkill and
budget for the land-use attorney. If this is a build-it-and-staff-it project
where you are not the daily presence, take Susquehanna and build by right.

Do not split the difference by buying in a Schuylkill township with no municipal
ordinance and assuming you are unzoned. **You are not.** The county ordinance
covers you.

## Before you tour anything

Run the three kill tests in `parcel-due-diligence.md`. Perc test, geology and
nitrate check, easement check. Any one fails, walk away. Touring parcels before
running these is how people fall in love with land they legally cannot build on.
