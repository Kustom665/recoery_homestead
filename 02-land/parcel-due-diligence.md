# Parcel Due Diligence

## The three kill tests

Run these on every candidate parcel **before you tour it, before you get
attached, and always before you spend money**. Any one fails, walk away.

### Kill test 0 — Who actually zones this parcel?

Cheapest test on the list — one email — so run it first.

A listing or a township secretary saying *"we don't have zoning here"* is **not
the answer.** Many Pennsylvania counties have adopted a countywide zoning
ordinance that automatically applies wherever a municipality has none.
Schuylkill County did exactly this, effective 27 Dec 2010. Fayette County
administers zoning for 31 of its 42 municipalities. In counties like these,
"unzoned township" land is still zoned — by the county.

Email the **county** planning office, not the township, and ask verbatim:

> For parcel [tax ID], in [township]: (1) does the municipality have its own
> zoning ordinance in effect, and (2) if not, does a countywide zoning ordinance
> apply to this parcel? If either applies, what is the parcel's zoning district
> and is a group home / recovery residence a permitted use, a conditional use,
> or not addressed?

Get it **in writing**. "Not addressed" is often better news than a permitted-use
listing, because it opens the FHA reasonable-accommodation route in
`03-zoning/` rather than a use-variance fight.

Fail condition: the parcel sits in a district where residential group living is
prohibited outright **and** the county has a history of denying accommodation
requests. That is a long, expensive road — not automatically fatal, but it
should knock the parcel down your list.

### Kill test 1 — Will it perc?

No septic, no dwellings, no project. Not harder. Over.

You need a **passing soil profile and perc test with both a primary and a
replacement area**, confirmed by the municipal Sewage Enforcement Officer.

Every offer includes a perc contingency. Every single one.

Details: `05-permits/sewage-act537.md`

### Kill test 2 — Geology and water

- **Is it underlain by carbonate geology (limestone)?** If yes, you likely lose
  the Act 537 exemption and face a full planning module — months and real money.
- **Is it within ¼ mile of water supplies with documented nitrate-nitrogen over
  5 ppm?** Same consequence.

Check DEP geologic mapping. This is a desk exercise you can do in an afternoon,
before you ever drive out.

### Kill test 3 — Easements

- **Conservation easement?**
- **Agricultural preservation easement?**

Either one is **permanent and runs with the land**. It prohibits the non-farm
residential development you are proposing. No amount of goodwill or legal work
undoes it.

This is especially critical in Lancaster, Chester, Berks, and Bucks, where
preservation programs are aggressive and a large share of attractive farm
parcels are already encumbered. The listing may not lead with it.

Title search plus attorney review. Non-negotiable.

## Full checklist

### Legal and title

- [ ] Deed reviewed by attorney
- [ ] Title search complete
- [ ] **No conservation or agricultural preservation easement**
- [ ] No right-of-way or access easements that interfere with the site plan
- [ ] No mineral, oil, or gas rights severed in a way that permits surface
      disturbance — this matters in western and northern PA
- [ ] No outstanding liens or back taxes
- [ ] Deed restrictions reviewed
- [ ] Clean-and-Green (Act 319) enrollment status checked — **rollback taxes**
      can be owed if the use changes

### Zoning and land use

- [ ] **Written** determination from the township: is there a zoning ordinance?
- [ ] Is there a subdivision and land development ordinance (SALDO)?
- [ ] Any other ordinances regulating construction or occupancy?
- [ ] Countywide zoning? (Greene, Fayette, Indiana have it; most counties don't)
- [ ] If zoned: what district, what uses are permitted by right, what requires
      special exception or conditional use?
- [ ] If zoned: any dispersion requirements, unrelated-occupancy caps, or
      restrictive "family" definitions? (These are FHA leverage — see
      `03-zoning/zoning-strategy.md`)

### Sewage and water

- [ ] SEO contacted, name and number on file
- [ ] Soil profile examination passed
- [ ] **Perc test passed — primary AND replacement areas**
- [ ] Municipal official plan shows the area as on-lot served
- [ ] Not carbonate geology
- [ ] Not within ¼ mile of nitrate-contaminated supplies
- [ ] Flow calculation done for target resident count
- [ ] Standard vs. alternate vs. community system determined
- [ ] Well feasibility — neighboring well depths and yields
- [ ] Water quality expectations for the area understood

### Site and access

- [ ] **Road frontage on a township road, not a state road** (avoids the
      PennDOT Highway Occupancy Permit, which is a long pole)
- [ ] Legal, deeded access — not a handshake right-of-way
- [ ] Electric service distance and cost to extend
- [ ] Cell and internet service — matters more than people expect for staff,
      telehealth, and residents' family contact
- [ ] Floodplain status (FEMA maps)
- [ ] Wetlands (USFWS National Wetlands Inventory, then field confirmation)
- [ ] Slope — steep ground is expensive ground
- [ ] Tillable acreage vs. wooded acreage
- [ ] Existing structures, and whether they are assets or demolition liabilities

### Funding fit

- [ ] Municipality population **≤20,000** (USDA Community Facilities eligibility)
- [ ] County has an active opioid settlement grant program
- [ ] County Single County Authority contacted, need documented
- [ ] County overdose data pulled for the grant narrative

### Neighbors and politics

- [ ] Natural buffer from adjacent dwellings
- [ ] Who are the immediate neighbors, and what is adjacent land used for?
- [ ] **Do not announce the project before you control the land.** Once
      neighbors organize, everything gets harder. This is the most common
      unforced error on projects like this.

## Sequence for evaluating candidates

1. **Desk screen** — county, zoning status, geology, easements, USDA
   eligibility, population. Costs nothing. Eliminates most parcels.
2. **Drive by** — access, slope, neighbors, general feel. Still costs nothing.
3. **Agreement of sale with a long due-diligence period** — this locks the
   parcel while you do the expensive work. Do not skip to step 4 without this.
4. **Spend money** — perc test, title search, attorney review, survey.
5. **Close, or walk.**

Most people do 2, fall in love, then do 3 and 4 under emotional pressure. Do 1
ruthlessly and you will look at a tenth as many parcels and buy a better one.

## What to bring to the SEO

When you call the Sewage Enforcement Officer, have ready: the parcel ID, the
approximate acreage, where on the parcel you intend to build, and your target
number of residents. That last one changes their answer — a 24-resident
community is a very different flow calculation than a house, and you want that
conversation early rather than after you have paid for drawings.
