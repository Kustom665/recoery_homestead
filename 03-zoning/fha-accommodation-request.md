# FHA Reasonable Accommodation Request — Draft Template

## How to use this

**Send it through your attorney.** A letter on law firm letterhead carries
several times the weight of the same letter from you, and the municipality's
solicitor will read it differently.

Rules:

1. **Always in writing.** Never verbal, never implied.
2. **Send certified mail, return receipt**, and copy the township solicitor.
3. **File it alongside your variance or conditional use application**, as a
   separate and explicit request — not folded into the application narrative.
4. **Keep every piece of correspondence.** If this ever becomes litigation, the
   paper trail is the case.
5. **Do not send this until you control the land** or have it under agreement.

The bracketed fields are yours to fill. The reasoning and the citations are the
part that matters — do not strip them out to sound friendlier.

## Template

> [DATE]
>
> **VIA CERTIFIED MAIL, RETURN RECEIPT REQUESTED**
>
> [Township Name] Board of Supervisors
> [Zoning Hearing Board]
> [Address]
>
> **cc:** [Township Solicitor]
>
> **RE: Request for Reasonable Accommodation under the Fair Housing Act
> — [Property Address / Tax Parcel ID]**
>
> Dear Members of the Board:
>
> This letter constitutes a formal request for reasonable accommodation under
> the Fair Housing Act, 42 U.S.C. § 3601 *et seq.*, and the Pennsylvania Human
> Relations Act, on behalf of [ORGANIZATION NAME], a Pennsylvania nonprofit
> corporation.
>
> **The proposed use**
>
> [ORGANIZATION NAME] proposes to establish a recovery residence at the
> above-referenced property. The residence will provide long-term housing for
> [NUMBER] adults in recovery from substance use disorder, who will live
> together as a single housekeeping unit, sharing meals, household
> responsibilities, and common areas in a family-style setting.
>
> Residents will live at the residence for approximately six months. The
> residence is a home, not a treatment facility. No clinical or medical
> treatment will be provided on site.
>
> **Residents are protected under the Fair Housing Act**
>
> Individuals recovering from substance use disorder who are not currently
> engaged in the illegal use of controlled substances are persons with
> handicaps within the meaning of 42 U.S.C. § 3602(h), and are therefore
> protected by the Fair Housing Act. This protection extends to municipal
> zoning and land use decisions.
>
> **The accommodation requested**
>
> [ORGANIZATION NAME] respectfully requests that the Township [CHOOSE AS
> APPLICABLE: waive / modify / interpret] [SPECIFIC ORDINANCE SECTION AND
> REQUIREMENT — for example: the definition of "family" at Section [X]; the
> limitation on the number of unrelated persons occupying a dwelling at Section
> [X]; the dispersion requirement at Section [X]] as applied to the proposed
> residence.
>
> **Why the accommodation is necessary**
>
> The requested accommodation is necessary to afford persons with disabilities
> an equal opportunity to use and enjoy a dwelling. Individuals in recovery
> from substance use disorder require a supportive, communal living environment
> in order to sustain recovery. Congregate living is not a preference; it is
> integral to the therapeutic benefit of the housing and to residents'
> ability to live successfully in a community setting.
>
> Applying the Township's [ordinance provision] to this residence would
> effectively prohibit persons with disabilities from living together in the
> mutually supportive arrangement their disability requires, while imposing no
> comparable restriction on households of similar size composed of persons
> without disabilities.
>
> **Why the accommodation is reasonable**
>
> The requested accommodation imposes no undue financial or administrative
> burden on the Township and would not fundamentally alter the nature of the
> Township's zoning scheme. The proposed residence is residential in character
> and in appearance. It generates [traffic / parking / utility] demand
> comparable to or less than [comparable permitted residential uses], as
> detailed in the enclosed materials.
>
> **Applicable authority**
>
> We respectfully direct the Board's attention to the following:
>
> - The United States commenced an action against the Township of Cheltenham,
>   Pennsylvania, alleging that its denial of a zoning variance violated the
>   Fair Housing Amendments Act. The matter was resolved in December 1997, with
>   the Township agreeing to amend its zoning ordinance to provide for the
>   granting of reasonable accommodations to individuals with handicaps.
> - In *Janush v. Township of Upper Southampton*, a dispersion requirement
>   precluding group homes within 1,000 feet of one another was held to violate
>   the Fair Housing Amendments Act.
>
> **Response requested**
>
> We respectfully request a written response within thirty (30) days. We are
> glad to meet with Township staff, provide additional information, or discuss
> reasonable alternatives that would accomplish the Township's legitimate
> objectives while affording our residents equal housing opportunity.
>
> We would much prefer to work cooperatively with the Township, and we approach
> this in that spirit.
>
> Respectfully,
>
> [NAME]
> [TITLE], [ORGANIZATION NAME]
>
> **Enclosures:** [Site plan; organizational documents; program description;
> letters of support]

## Notes on using it well

**Tone is doing real work here.** The letter is cooperative in register and
unmistakable in substance. That combination is deliberate. A hostile letter
hardens the board; a vague one gets ignored; this one gives them an easy path to
yes and makes the alternative visible without threatening.

**Enclose letters of support.** From the county Single County Authority, from
treatment providers, from clergy, from anyone locally credible. A board that
sees local backing behaves differently than one that sees an outsider.

**"Single housekeeping unit" is not decoration.** Many PA ordinances define
"family" in terms of a single housekeeping unit. Using the ordinance's own
language positions the residence within the existing definition rather than
asking for an exception to it — sometimes you win on interpretation without
needing the accommodation at all.

**Ask for both.** File the variance or conditional use application *and* this
request. If the board denies the variance, the accommodation request is already
on the record and preserved for review.

**If they deny or stall past 30 days**, contacts in `07-action/contacts.md`:
Disability Rights Pennsylvania, the Pennsylvania Human Relations Commission, the
Housing Equality Center of Pennsylvania, and the DOJ Civil Rights Division
Housing Section.

## The thing to remember

Most townships are not acting out of malice. They are acting out of unfamiliarity
and a fear of what neighbors will say. A clear, calm, well-documented request
from an organization that has obviously done its homework resolves a large share
of these before anyone gets near a courtroom.

Lead with cooperation. Keep the record clean anyway.
