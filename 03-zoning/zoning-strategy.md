# Zoning Strategy

Two paths. Pick the first if you possibly can.

---

## Path A — Buy where there is no zoning

**822 Pennsylvania municipalities have no zoning ordinance.** In those
townships there is no permitted-use table, no variance process, no zoning
hearing board, and no neighbor with standing to object to your use.

This is not a loophole. Pennsylvania municipalities are not required to adopt
zoning, and most rural ones never did.

### What still applies in an unzoned township

Do not confuse "no zoning" with "no rules." These are state-level and follow you
everywhere:

| Still applies | Authority |
| --- | --- |
| Building code | PA Uniform Construction Code (UCC), statewide |
| Sewage / septic | Act 537, PA DEP, enforced by the municipal SEO |
| Well and water | PA DEP |
| Stormwater and erosion | County Conservation District, DEP Chapter 102 |
| Driveway access onto a state road | PennDOT Highway Occupancy Permit |
| Recovery house licensing | DDAP, 28 Pa. Code Ch. 717 |
| Fair housing | Federal FHA, PA Human Relations Act |

Some unzoned townships also have **standalone ordinances** — a subdivision and
land development ordinance (SALDO), a junkyard ordinance, a nuisance ordinance.
PSATS has warned townships against using standalone ordinances as de facto
zoning, but they exist. Ask the township secretary directly:

> "Does the township have a zoning ordinance? Does it have a subdivision and
> land development ordinance? Are there any other ordinances that regulate what
> can be built or how property is occupied?"

Get the answer in writing. A township secretary's email is cheap insurance.

### How to verify before you offer

1. Call the **township secretary**. Ask the three questions above.
2. Call the **county planning commission**. Confirm whether the county has
   countywide zoning (Greene, Fayette, and Indiana do; most do not).
3. Search the municipality on **eCode360** or **General Code** to see what
   ordinances are published.
4. Have your attorney confirm. This is a 30-minute task for them and it
   protects a six-figure purchase.

## Path B — Zoned municipality, using the Fair Housing Act

If you end up somewhere zoned — which is nearly certain in Schuylkill, Berks, or
anywhere in southeastern PA — this is your strategy. It is stronger than most
people realize.

### The legal foundation

People in recovery from substance use disorder are **disabled** under the Fair
Housing Act, so long as they are not currently using illegal drugs. The FHA
prohibits discrimination in zoning decisions relating to residential uses, and
requires housing providers and municipalities to make **reasonable
accommodations** — changes in rules, policies, practices, or services when
necessary to give a person with a disability equal opportunity to use and enjoy
a dwelling.

### Pennsylvania precedent

- **United States v. Township of Cheltenham** — the U.S. sued the township in
  1997 for denying a zoning variance in violation of the Fair Housing
  Amendments Act. Settled December 1997; the township agreed to **amend its
  zoning ordinance** to provide for granting reasonable accommodations to
  people with disabilities.
- **Janush v. Township of Upper Southampton** — a dispersion requirement
  barring group homes within 1,000 feet of each other was held to violate the
  FHAA.

These are your township's precedent too. Their solicitor knows these cases.

### Variance vs. reasonable accommodation — know the difference

This is the part most applicants get wrong.

| | Zoning variance | FHA reasonable accommodation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard | Unnecessary hardship, unique to the property, hardship not self-created | Accommodation is **necessary** to give people with disabilities equal opportunity, and is **reasonable** |
| Who decides | Zoning hearing board | The municipality — and a denial is reviewable in federal court |
| Burden on you | Heavy, and PA variance law is strict | Substantially lighter |
| Municipality's exposure if it denies wrongly | Reversal | **Federal FHA liability, damages, attorney's fees, possible DOJ involvement** |

**Ask for both, in the same submission.** File the variance application and,
separately and explicitly, a written request for reasonable accommodation. If
the board denies the variance, the accommodation request is already on the
record and preserved.

Never let the request be verbal. Never let it be implied. Write it, date it,
send it certified, and copy the solicitor. Template in
`fha-accommodation-request.md`.

### Language discipline

How you describe the project changes the legal frame. Be accurate — and be
deliberate.

**Use:** residence, home, residents, family-style household, individuals with
disabilities in recovery, single housekeeping unit.

**Avoid:** facility, clients, patients, program participants, treatment center,
campus, beds, intake.

"Facility" and "clients" invite an institutional-use classification.
"Residence" and "residents" support a residential-use classification, which is
what you want under both the ordinance and the FHA. This is not spin — a
recovery residence genuinely is a home, not a clinic. Describe it as the thing
it is.

### Watch for these ordinance provisions

Each has been challenged successfully somewhere:

- **Dispersion / spacing requirements** (e.g. no group home within 1,000 feet
  of another) — this is exactly what fell in *Janush*
- **Occupancy caps on unrelated persons** — a common FHA target
- **Definitions of "family"** that exclude unrelated adults living as a single
  household
- **Special exception or conditional use requirements** applied only to group
  homes and not to comparable residential uses
- **Public hearing requirements** that exist only for disability-related housing

Flag any of these to your attorney immediately. They are leverage.

### Backup

- **Disability Rights Pennsylvania** — the state's protection and advocacy
  organization; publishes guidance on discriminatory zoning and the FHA
- **Pennsylvania Human Relations Commission** — state-level fair housing
  enforcement
- **Housing Equality Center of Pennsylvania** — fair housing enforcement and
  guidance
- **U.S. DOJ Civil Rights Division, Housing Section** — brought *Cheltenham*

## Practical sequencing

1. **Do not announce the project before you control the land.** Once neighbors
   organize, everything gets harder — this is the single most common unforced
   error.
2. **Buy in an unzoned township if you can.** Path A removes the entire fight.
3. **Get a written zoning determination** from the township before closing.
   Make the purchase contingent on it, alongside the perc test.
4. **Retain a PA land-use attorney before you file anything.** Not after the
   first denial. Their first letter carries more weight than your third.
5. **If you must go through a hearing**, show up with the reasonable
   accommodation request already filed, neighbors already met, and a site plan
   that looks like homes rather than an institution.
