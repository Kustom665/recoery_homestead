# Permit Sequence

The order matters. Each step gates the next, and doing them out of order is how
people burn money on drawings for land they cannot build on.

## Before you own the land

These happen during the due-diligence period of an agreement of sale. All of
them are purchase contingencies.

| # | Step | Who | Typical time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Written zoning determination** — is the use permitted? Is there zoning at all? | Township secretary / zoning officer | 1–3 weeks |
| 2 | **Deed and easement search** — conservation or ag preservation easements? | Title company + attorney | 2–3 weeks |
| 3 | **Soil profile + perc test**, primary and replacement | Municipal SEO | 2–6 weeks, seasonal |
| 4 | **Carbonate geology and nitrate check** | DEP mapping + attorney | Days |
| 5 | **Well feasibility** — neighboring well depths and yields | Local driller | 1–2 weeks |
| 6 | **Confirm USDA rural eligibility** for the municipality | USDA RD PA office | Days |
| 7 | **Confirm floodplain and wetlands status** | County Conservation District, FEMA maps, USFWS wetlands mapper | 1–2 weeks |

**Any of 1–4 fails, walk away.** That is the entire point of the contingency
period. Walking away from a bad parcel is a win, not a loss.

## After you close

| # | Step | Who | Typical time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8 | **Act 537 planning module or exemption** | DEP + municipality | Exemption: weeks. Module: 3–9 months. |
| 9 | **Septic system design and permit** | Licensed designer + SEO | 1–3 months |
| 10 | **Well permit and drilling** | Driller, DEP | 1–2 months |
| 11 | **Erosion and sedimentation control plan** — required for most earth disturbance | County Conservation District | 1–3 months |
| 12 | **Stormwater management plan** if disturbance thresholds are met | Engineer + municipality | 1–3 months |
| 13 | **Driveway permit** — PennDOT Highway Occupancy Permit if fronting a state road; township permit otherwise | PennDOT or township | 1–4 months (PennDOT is slower) |
| 14 | **Land development / subdivision approval** if the municipality has a SALDO | Municipal planning commission | 3–9 months |
| 15 | **Zoning permit / variance / conditional use** if zoned | Zoning officer or hearing board | 2–6 months |
| 16 | **UCC building permits** — one per structure | Municipal code office or third-party agency | 2–8 weeks each |
| 17 | **Temporary use permit** for construction-phase campers | Township | 2–6 weeks |
| 18 | **Inspections** throughout construction | Code official | Ongoing |
| 19 | **Certificate of occupancy** per structure | Code official | At completion |
| 20 | **DDAP recovery house license** | DDAP | Apply well before opening |

## The critical path

The long poles, in order of how much they can delay you:

1. **Act 537 planning module** (if you fail the exemption) — up to 9 months.
   This alone is worth choosing a non-carbonate parcel for.
2. **Land development approval** (if the municipality has a SALDO) — up to 9
   months.
3. **PennDOT Highway Occupancy Permit** (if on a state road) — slow and
   unpredictable. **Prefer a parcel with township road frontage.**
4. **Zoning variance or conditional use** (if zoned) — months, plus the
   political risk.

**A parcel in an unzoned township, on a township road, with a passing perc test
on non-carbonate ground, skips or shortens all four.** That is the entire
argument for the county shortlist in `02-land/`, restated as a schedule.

## Building code notes

**The UCC applies statewide.** "No zoning" does not mean "no building code."
Enforcement is by the municipality or by a **third-party agency** the
municipality contracts with — in some municipalities, by state L&I.

Two exemptions worth knowing:

**Agricultural buildings** get UCC exemptions that dwellings do not. This is
real, and it is why Phase 3 of the site plan is cheaper per square foot than
Phases 1 and 2. Barns, greenhouses, and equipment buildings are the cheap
square footage on this project.

**Recreational cabins** have a UCC exemption under specific conditions —
generally seasonal use, not rented, and limits around plumbing and potable
water. **This will not help you.** A recovery residence is full-time occupancy
with plumbing. Mentioned here specifically so you do not spend a month chasing
it, because it comes up constantly in tiny-home forums.

**Get the occupancy classification in writing early.** R-3 versus R-2 or I-1 is
the biggest single cost variable in the project. See `04-architecture/site-plan.md`.

## Who you need on the team

Hire these in Phase 0. Trying to save money here costs more later.

| Role | What they do | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **PA land-use attorney** | Zoning determination, easement review, FHA strategy, entity structure, labor-law question | Before you make an offer |
| **PA-licensed architect or engineer** | Stamped drawings for the community building and anything non-simple-residential | Before permits |
| **Septic designer** | System design, flow calculations, coordination with SEO | Before site plan is final |
| **Surveyor** | Boundary survey, site plan base, subdivision plan if needed | After closing |
| **Well driller** | Feasibility, drilling, yield and potability testing | Phase 0 |
| **Third-party code agency** | Plan review and inspections, if the municipality uses one | At permit stage |

## Realistic timeline

Land under agreement to first residents on site:

| Scenario | Timeline |
| --- | --- |
| **Best case** — unzoned township, township road, perc passes, Act 537 exemption, campers under temporary use | **6–9 months** |
| **Typical** — some zoning work, land development review, no major surprises | **12–18 months** |
| **Hard case** — full planning module, variance fight, PennDOT permit | **24–36 months** |

The gap between best case and hard case is almost entirely determined by
**which parcel you buy**. That is why land selection gets the most rigor in this
package, and why the three kill tests in `02-land/parcel-due-diligence.md` are
worth running on every candidate before you tour it.
