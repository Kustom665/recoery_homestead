# Budget Model

## Read this first

**These are modeled planning ranges, not quotes.** They exist so you can size a
grant request and know roughly what you are walking into. Every number needs to
be replaced with a real bid before you commit.

Assumptions baked in:

- Rural Pennsylvania county from the Tier 1/2 shortlist, not southeastern PA
- 30 acres
- Build-out to 8 cottages / 16 residents in the first three phases
- Significant resident and volunteer labor on non-licensed work
- 2026 costs, which have been volatile — treat lumber and concrete as the
  most uncertain lines

**Per resident per year:** roughly **$18,700 – $31,700**
**Per resident per day:** roughly **$51 – $87**

That per-day figure is your most important number in a grant application. It
compares extremely favorably to inpatient treatment, incarceration, or repeated
emergency department visits — and that comparison is the argument that wins
funding. Pull your county's actual numbers for the contrast.

## Revenue

| Source | Range | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Resident fees | $0 – $150,000 | $0–$800/month per resident. Many residents have no income on arrival. Chapter 717 regulates how you handle resident finances. |
| County opioid settlement (operating) | $50,000 – $250,000 | Often renewable annually |
| PHARE | Varies | More capital-oriented than operating |
| SCA contract / per-diem | Varies | If you become a contracted provider — this is the most stable operating revenue available |
| Farm sales | $0 – $30,000 | **Triggers the FLSA analysis. Talk to your attorney before planning any farm revenue.** |
| Individual donations and churches | $10,000 – $75,000 | Grows with time and visibility |
| Foundations | Varies | Year two and beyond |

**The realistic operating model is a blend of grants and resident fees.** The
"SCA contract / per-diem" row above is kept for completeness, but the 23 Oct
2026 verification round found **no standard SCA per-diem** for recovery houses
in Pennsylvania — DDAP routes money through SCAs as grants. Treat per-diem as
zero until an SCA puts a rate in writing. That makes this operating budget a
fundraising budget. The for-profit alternative, where resident fees carry the
house, is modeled in `08-decision/entity-and-financials.md`.

## How to phase the money

1. **Phase 0** — county opioid settlement capital grant, foundations, and
   donations. **Not USDA**: the Community Facilities loan requires a
   feasibility study and repayment history a start-up does not have (see the
   correction box in `funding-stack.md`). This is the hardest money to raise
   because there is nothing to show for it — which is why
   `08-decision/ceo-decision.md` recommends earning the track record in a
   leased house first.
2. **Phase 1** — PHARE plus settlement funds. Now you have something visible,
   which makes everything after this easier.
3. **Phase 2** — PHARE, settlement, foundations. Sweat equity meaningfully
   reduces this phase.
4. **Phase 3** — Agricultural grants, donations, in-kind. USDA has separate
   agricultural programs worth exploring here.
5. **Operating** — SCA contract, settlement operating grants, resident fees,
   donations.

**Get the capital stack committed before you close on land.** An agreement of
sale with a long due-diligence period locks the parcel while you assemble it.

## The single biggest cost variable

**Occupancy classification.** R-3 versus R-2 or I-1.

If the community building or any housing structure gets classified as
institutional, add fire sprinklers, commercial egress, rated assemblies, and
full ADA compliance. **That can add $150,000–$400,000+** to the numbers above.

This is why `04-architecture/site-plan.md` insists on a cottage cluster rather
than a bunkhouse, and why you get the classification in writing from the code
official before you pay for drawings.

Everything else on this page is an estimate. That one is a cliff.
