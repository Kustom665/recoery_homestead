# Funding Stack

Every named program, what it actually pays for, and what gates it.

> **Corrections from the 23 Oct 2026 verification round** — read before the rest.
>
> - **USDA Community Facilities is not a Phase 0 source.** Start-up applicants
>   normally need a third-party feasibility study, must show they cannot get
>   commercial credit, and must show repayment capacity from revenues. For an
>   organization with no operating history that is a year-two-plus source. The
>   "best-kept secret" framing below oversold it.
> - **No standard SCA per-diem was found.** DDAP funds recovery houses through
>   grants routed via SCAs, not a published daily rate. Do not build an
>   operating budget on a per-diem until an SCA puts one in writing.
> - **DDAP's March 2025 $2M round (up to $500k each) went to five existing
>   operators** to link people into existing licensed houses. State money
>   follows a track record; it does not create one.
> - **Licensure is required only when you take state/federal money or
>   state-agency referrals.** A private-pay house with private referrals can
>   open before the license lands. A 2026 bill would change that within three
>   years.

## The gating item

**Almost all of this requires a 501(c)(3).** That is the first move, not the
fifth. Nothing else in this document unlocks without it.

If you want to chase money before your own determination letter lands, use a
**fiscal sponsor** — an existing 501(c)(3) that accepts grants on your behalf
for a fee (typically 5–10% of funds). Your county's Single County Authority or
an established recovery nonprofit can often point you to one.

A common structure worth discussing with your attorney: **a nonprofit operator
paired with an LLC that holds the land.** It separates liability and keeps
options open. Do not improvise this — get it designed properly the first time.

## Tier 1 — Go after these first

### County opioid settlement funds

**The best fit in the entire stack.** Recovery housing is explicitly an
eligible use.

- Pennsylvania's settlement allocation: **70% to counties**, 15% to the
  Commonwealth, 15% to litigating subdivisions
- Counties may only spend it on addiction prevention, treatment, and recovery
- Overseen by the **Pennsylvania Opioid Misuse and Addiction Abatement Trust**
  (paopioidtrust.org), which approves and monitors local spending

Live county programs confirmed:

| County | Program | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Montgomery | Competitive Opioid Remediation Application | **$59.7M** total; Year Two performance period is CY2026 |
| Bucks | Opioid Settlement Funds program | Overview published by the county |
| Berks | Via **Council on Chemical Abuse** | Berks funding request process |
| Delaware | Opioid Settlement Grant Initiatives | Has funded 40+ programs including **housing** |
| Dauphin | Opioid Remediation Grant Program | ~$13M over 18 years |
| York | Via York County Planning Commission | |

Whatever county you build in will have one. **Find its opioid settlement
coordinator and call them before you write anything.** These programs are
actively looking for credible applicants and often have more money than good
proposals.

### USDA Rural Development — Community Facilities Direct Loan & Grant

**The best-kept secret in this plan.** USDA explicitly lists **transitional
housing** as an eligible "essential community facility."

| Feature | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Who is eligible | Public bodies, **private nonprofits**, tribes |
| Where | Rural areas — municipalities of **no more than 20,000 residents** |
| Eligible uses | Purchase, construct, improve facilities; buy equipment; related project costs |
| Loan terms | Up to **40 years**, no fees, **no prepayment penalty**, **no prevailing wage requirement** |
| Grant | Applications are considered **loan-first**; grants max out at **$50,000**, awarded on financial need |

The grant is small. **The loan is the point** — 40-year money with no fees and
no prevailing wage requirement is better than anything a bank will offer you,
and it is specifically designed for exactly this.

This is a second, independent reason to build in a rural county. Bucks and
Montgomery mostly fail the 20,000-population test.

Start with the **USDA Rural Development Pennsylvania state office**.

### PHFA — PHARE (PA Housing Affordability and Rehabilitation Enhancement Fund)

- Pennsylvania's housing trust fund, established under Act 105
- **$85M+** available following the 2024–25 budget, nearly doubling the state's
  commitment
- Has funded **~400 initiatives across all 67 counties**
- Eligible uses include **creation of rental housing**, preservation and
  rehabilitation, homelessness prevention, and explicitly **"innovative housing
  solutions"** — which is the door your project walks through
- Applications through **phare.phfa.org**; the deadline has historically fallen
  around **November 20 at 2:00 PM**

Confirm the current cycle's deadline directly with PHFA — do not rely on last
year's date.

## Tier 2 — Real money, more process

### SAMHSA

Federal grants supporting recovery housing and recovery community
organizations. Larger, more competitive, longer lead times, heavier reporting.
Worth pursuing in year two once you have an operating track record.

### CDBG — Community Development Block Grant

Flows through your county. Can fund acquisition and infrastructure. Talk to the
county community development office. Often overlooked because people assume
it's only for municipalities.

### HUD Continuum of Care

Applicable if you serve people experiencing homelessness — and there is heavy
overlap between that population and early recovery. Requires joining your
regional CoC, which is worth doing anyway for the referral relationships.

### County Single County Authority (SCA)

Every PA county has one — the local drug and alcohol commission. In Berks it is
the **Council on Chemical Abuse**.

The SCA is less a funding source than **the relationship that determines
whether your beds fill**. They control referrals. A recovery residence with no
referral pipeline is an empty building with a mortgage.

**Meet your SCA before you buy land.** Ask what they are short of. If they say
"we have nowhere to send men who need six months," you have just validated your
entire model — and you have a letter of support for every grant application you
will ever write.

## The licensure interlock

Read this twice.

**DDAP recovery house licensure is required if you accept state or federal
funding *or* referrals from state agencies** — Act 59 of 2017, implemented at
**28 Pa. Code Chapter 717**, effective December 2021.

Every Tier 1 and Tier 2 source above is state or federal money. The SCA
relationship is state referrals.

**So licensure is mandatory for your model.** There is no version of this plan
that funds itself with government money and skips licensure.

Additionally, a 2026 bill from **Sen. Tracy Pennycuick** would require **all**
established PA recovery residences to obtain licensure within three years.

Practical consequence: **Chapter 717 carries physical-plant requirements**, so
the buildings have to be designed backward from the licensing standard. Read
Ch. 717 *before* you finalize any floor plan. Details in
`01-model/entity-and-licensure.md`.

## Suggested sequence

1. **Now** — Form the 501(c)(3), or line up a fiscal sponsor.
2. **Now** — Call the SCA in your target county. Get the need documented and
   ask for a letter of support.
3. **Now** — Call the county opioid settlement coordinator. Ask about the next
   application window.
4. **Before you close on land** — Confirm USDA Rural Development eligibility for
   the specific municipality.
5. **Next PHARE cycle** — Apply, with the SCA letter attached.
6. **Year two** — SAMHSA, CDBG, CoC, once you have a track record.

**Get the money committed before you close on land, not after.** A signed
agreement of sale with a long due-diligence period is your friend here — it
locks the parcel while you assemble the capital stack.
