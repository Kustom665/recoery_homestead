# Core Grant Narrative — Draft

Adapt this for each application. The structure holds across county opioid
settlement funds, PHARE, USDA, SAMHSA, and foundations — swap the emphasis, keep
the bones.

**Fill every bracket with real numbers.** Reviewers can tell instantly when a
narrative is generic, and generic narratives lose to specific ones.

---

## The need

> [COUNTY] County recorded [NUMBER] overdose deaths in [YEAR], a rate of
> [RATE] per 100,000 residents — [above/among the highest in] the Pennsylvania
> average. [ADD: local detail — a specific hospital's ED numbers, the county
> drug court's caseload, the SCA's waitlist.]
>
> Detoxification and short-term inpatient treatment are available in the region.
> What is not available is what comes next. Individuals completing detox or a
> 28-day program are routinely discharged with nowhere stable to go — frequently
> back to the housing, relationships, and conditions in which their substance
> use developed. [SCA NAME] reports [SPECIFIC GAP — e.g., "no long-term
> recovery housing for men in the county," or "a waitlist of X"].
>
> The gap is not treatment. The gap is time and stable housing after treatment.

**Make this section specific and local.** Pull county overdose data from
OverdoseFreePA and the Center for Rural Pennsylvania. Quote the SCA directly —
with permission, by name.

## The response

> [ORGANIZATION] will establish [PROJECT NAME], a [NUMBER]-resident recovery
> community on [ACREAGE] acres in [TOWNSHIP], [COUNTY] County. Residents will
> live on site for six months in a structured, abstinence-supportive community
> organized around three things: peer community, daily purposeful work on a
> working farm, and connection to established recovery programming.
>
> The model is drawn from Jacob's Ladder at Brookside Farm in Aurora, West
> Virginia — a long-term, farm-based residential recovery program documented in
> the 2018 film *Recovery Boys*. Daily agricultural work is not vocational
> filler; it is the therapeutic core. Physical, repetitive, outdoor work with
> visible results and genuine consequence — animals that must be fed, crops
> that must be harvested — rebuilds the routine, responsibility, and sense of
> contribution that sustained substance use erodes.
>
> Residents will live in individual cottages arranged around a shared community
> building, in a residential setting designed to feel like a home rather than an
> institution. [ORGANIZATION] will pursue licensure under 28 Pa. Code Chapter
> 717 and certification through the Pennsylvania affiliate of the National
> Alliance for Recovery Residences.

---

## What makes this different

> **Length.** Six months, not twenty-eight days. Most relapse occurs in the
> window that short-term programs discharge people into.
>
> **Work with consequence.** Residents operate a working farm and participate in
> constructing the community itself. Early residents build the cottages that
> later residents will live in. Men who have spent years taking leave having
> built something permanent.
>
> **A transition tier.** Rather than discharging residents off a cliff at month
> six, [PROJECT NAME] includes a graduated transition — increased independence
> and responsibility while remaining connected to the community.
>
> **Rural siting.** Distance from the conditions and networks associated with
> active use, combined with land that makes the agricultural model possible.

## Budget and sustainability

> Total capital cost for Phases 0 through 3 is projected at $[AMOUNT], including
> land acquisition, site infrastructure, a community building and bath house,
> [NUMBER] resident cottages, and agricultural infrastructure. [ORGANIZATION]
> requests $[AMOUNT] from [FUNDER] toward [SPECIFIC PHASE OR LINE ITEM].
>
> Annual operating cost at full capacity is projected at $[AMOUNT], or
> approximately $[AMOUNT] per resident per day. Operating revenue will derive
> from [resident fees / SCA contract / settlement operating grants / donations],
> with the objective of a contracted per-diem relationship with [SCA] providing
> the sustaining base.
>
> By comparison, [COUNTY] County's cost for [inpatient treatment / incarceration
> / repeated emergency department utilization] is approximately $[AMOUNT] per
> person per day.

**That final comparison is the argument that wins funding.** Get the real local
numbers. A per-day cost that undercuts incarceration or inpatient care makes the
case in one line.

## Outcomes

> [ORGANIZATION] will track and report:
>
> - Residents served
> - Program completion rate
> - Housing status at 6 and 12 months post-discharge
> - Employment or education status at 6 and 12 months
> - Recovery status at 6 and 12 months
> - Recidivism, for residents referred through the justice system
> - Emergency department utilization, pre- and post-program, where data-sharing
>   permits

**Commit to fewer measures and actually collect them.** A funder would rather
see five metrics reported reliably for three years than fifteen promised and
never delivered. Build the data collection into intake from day one — retrofitting
it is painful.

---

## Notes on writing these

**Lead with the gap, not with your idea.** Funders fund needs, not visions. The
first paragraph should make a reviewer who has never met you understand why the
absence of this is a problem.

**Be specific about the county.** A narrative that could describe anywhere reads
as a template, and reviewers see dozens of templates.

**Do not oversell.** Claiming a 90% success rate in early recovery reads as
naive and damages credibility with anyone who knows the field. Sober,
evidence-grounded claims land better.

**Name the model.** *Recovery Boys* and Jacob's Ladder give reviewers something
concrete to picture, and it signals that you did not invent this in a vacuum.

**Attach the SCA letter to every single application.**
