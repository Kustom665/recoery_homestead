# 90-Day Plan

Nothing here requires you to own land or have money. Every item is a phone call,
a form, or a conversation. The point of the first 90 days is to become the kind
of applicant funders say yes to.

---

## Weeks 1–2 — Legal foundation

- [ ] **Find and retain a PA nonprofit / land-use attorney.** Interview two or
      three. Ask directly whether they have done recovery housing or group home
      zoning work. This is the most important hire in the project.
- [ ] Bring the attorney three questions on day one:
      1. Entity structure — straight 501(c)(3), or nonprofit operator plus
         landholding LLC?
      2. The **FLSA / resident labor question** — how do we structure sweat
         equity safely?
      3. What do they need from you to start incorporation?
- [ ] **Incorporate the PA nonprofit corporation.**
- [ ] Draft bylaws.
- [ ] Obtain the EIN.

## Weeks 2–4 — Board and identity

- [ ] **Recruit 5–7 board members.** Target: lived recovery experience, a
      clinician, real estate or construction, finance or accounting, and someone
      connected to your target county. Funders read your board.
- [ ] Hold the first board meeting, adopt bylaws, elect officers.
- [ ] Write the one-page project description. You will use it in every
      conversation for the next two years.
- [ ] Open the bank account.
- [ ] File **IRS Form 1023** (or 1023-EZ if you qualify).
- [ ] Register with the PA Bureau of Charitable Organizations.

## Weeks 3–6 — Relationships that decide whether this works

This is the highest-leverage block in the 90 days. Do not skip it to go look at
land.

- [ ] **Call the Single County Authority** in your top two target counties. Ask:
      *"What are you short of? Where do you have nowhere to send people?"*
      Listen more than you talk.
- [ ] Ask the SCA for a **letter of support**. You will attach it to every grant
      application you ever file.
- [ ] **Call the county opioid settlement coordinator** in both counties. Ask
      when the next application window opens and what they have funded so far.
- [ ] **Call DDAP licensing.** Ask what goes wrong in new recovery house
      applications. Free, and it will save you months.
- [ ] **Get 28 Pa. Code Chapter 717** and read it end to end. Take notes on
      every physical-plant requirement — those become architectural constraints.
- [ ] Contact **USDA Rural Development PA state office**. Confirm Community
      Facilities eligibility criteria and what a pre-application looks like.
- [ ] Contact **PHFA** about the current PHARE cycle and deadline.
- [ ] Reach out to the **PA NARR affiliate** about certification.
- [ ] If your 1023 will take a while: identify a **fiscal sponsor**.

## Weeks 5–8 — Narrow the county

- [ ] Score your candidates against the four axes in
      `02-land/county-shortlist.md`.
- [ ] Pull **county overdose data** for the grant narrative.
- [ ] Confirm which townships in your top county **have no zoning ordinance**.
      Call township secretaries directly. Get answers in writing.
- [ ] Check **DEP carbonate geology mapping** across the county.
- [ ] Confirm municipality populations against the **USDA 20,000 threshold**.
- [ ] **Pick one county.** Commit. Scattering across three counties is how the
      next six months disappear.

## Weeks 7–10 — Land search

- [ ] Set up saved searches: Land.com, LandWatch, county GIS, local realtors,
      tax sale listings.
- [ ] Ask the county about **surplus or tax-sale parcels**, and about municipal
      or church land that might be donated.
- [ ] **Desk-screen** every candidate: zoning status, geology, easements, USDA
      eligibility, road frontage. Costs nothing, eliminates most.
- [ ] Drive by the survivors.
- [ ] Get the **SEO's name and number** for the townships you are targeting.
- [ ] Shortlist 3–5 parcels.

## Weeks 9–12 — Money and momentum

- [ ] Write the **core grant narrative** — `06-money/grant-narrative.md` is the
      starting draft. Everything after this is adaptation.
- [ ] Assemble the standard grant attachment packet: incorporation documents,
      IRS determination or 1023 receipt, board list with bios, budget, SCA
      letter of support, project description.
- [ ] **Submit the first application** — most likely county opioid settlement
      funds, since those cycles run most frequently.
- [ ] Put an **agreement of sale with a long due-diligence period** on your top
      parcel. This locks it while you do the expensive work and raise capital.
- [ ] Order the **perc test and soil profile**.
- [ ] Order the **title search and easement review**.
- [ ] Get a **written zoning determination** from the township.

---

## At day 90, you should have

- A Pennsylvania nonprofit corporation with a real board
- Form 1023 filed
- A relationship with the SCA and a letter of support in hand
- A relationship with the county opioid settlement coordinator
- Chapter 717 read, with its physical-plant requirements noted
- One county chosen
- A parcel under agreement with due diligence running
- At least one grant application submitted

None of that requires capital. All of it is what separates people who build this
from people who talk about building it for three years.

---

## The three things most likely to go wrong

**1. You buy land before you have the money lined up.** Then you own dirt with a
carrying cost and no path forward. Get the capital stack committed first; use a
long due-diligence period to hold the parcel while you do.

**2. You skip the SCA relationship and build a beautiful empty compound.**
Referrals determine occupancy. Occupancy determines survival. Make this call in
week 3.

**3. You announce the project before you control the land.** Neighbors organize,
the township hardens, and a straightforward approval becomes a two-year fight.
Keep it quiet until the deed is in hand or the agreement is signed.

---

## What to do in week one, specifically

If you do nothing else on this page, do these three:

1. Call two land-use attorneys and book consultations.
2. Call the Single County Authority in Schuylkill or Fayette and ask what they
   are short of.
3. Download 28 Pa. Code Chapter 717 and start reading it.

All three are free. All three change what you build.
