# CEO Decision — Would You Write the Check?

Written 23 Oct 2026. This is the owner's-money read of the whole package,
produced by running the business-builder workflow against the 19 documents in
this repo and four rounds of fresh verification. It does not replace those
documents. It tells you which of them to act on first, and it changes some
numbers in them.

**The one-line answer:** Do not buy land first. Open a licensed six-to-eight
bed recovery house in a leased farmhouse within 90 minutes of Berks, get it
full and licensed, and let that track record buy the farm in year two. The
Recovery Boys compound is the year-two-to-three build, not the year-one move.

---

## 1. The objective, stated so it can be corrected

Nothing in this repo says how much of your own money you can put at risk, and
that number controls everything below.

**Working assumption:** you can personally risk **$5,000–$25,000** over the next
90 days, you want **first revenue inside 120 days**, and you want to **own
something** at the end rather than be a salaried employee of a board. That
assumption comes from Nye AI Solutions being a solo operator selling $3,500
site builds. **If it is off by 10× in either direction, say so now** — the
recommendation flips.

Winning, under that assumption: a licensed recovery house with six paying
residents, cash-flow positive, by day 120, that an SCA will refer into.

## 3. Three paths that are actually different

### Path A — The compound as written (buy raw land, build cottages)

The existing `04-architecture` plan. 20–40 acres, bath house, community
building, eight cottages. **$1.29–2.90M** all-in per `06-money/budget-model.md`.

- **Who pays:** grants to a 501(c)(3). F4 and F5 say those grants follow a track
  record you do not have.
- **Time to first resident:** 18–36 months, and only if the money lands.
- **Your ownership:** none. You would be a salaried executive director.
- **Verdict:** this is a year-three destination, not a year-one plan. Keep the
  documents; do not act on them first.

### Path B — Buy a farmhouse with acreage, license the house, build later

Buy one of the F9/F10 comparables ($175–300k + rehab). License the farmhouse
under Ch. 717 (F2 — no CO, 60 sq ft/resident; an 8-bedroom 1876 farmhouse is a
12–14 bed house on paper). Open with 6–8 residents in the house. Cottages come
from revenue and grants in years two and three.

- **Who pays:** you need **$45–75k down** plus closing on a $250k purchase, or
  seller financing, or an investor. Conventional lenders are cold on
  sober-living use; USDA is out until you have a track record (F5).
- **Time to first resident:** 6–9 months (purchase + rehab + license).
- **Your ownership:** full, via an LLC.
- **Verdict:** the right year-two move. It needs capital you have not said you
  have, which is the honest reason it is not the year-one move.

### Path C — Lease a house, open in 90 days, buy with the track record

Form a PA LLC. Lease a 4–5 bedroom house (farmhouse if one is available at the
right price, ordinary house if not) within 90 minutes of Berks. Furnish six to
eight beds. Hire a live-in house manager. Open private-pay, apply for the DDAP
license in parallel, and go to the SCA the day it lands.

- **Who pays:** you, **$8,000–$14,000**, itemized in section 7.
- **Time to first resident:** **60–90 days.** Breakeven at **5 residents**.
- **Your ownership:** full.
- **What it is not:** it is not the farm. It is six men in a house with a
  schedule, chores, meetings, and a manager. The farm is what the house earns
  you the right to build.
- **Verdict:** the one an owner would write the check for.

Harvest Hosts sits inside Path C as a $99 **scouting tool**: a year of free
overnight stays at ~398 PA farms is the cheapest way there is to sleep on
working farms in Wyoming, Bradford, and Susquehanna counties, meet owners, and
find the Utterback-style partner that Jacob's Ladder actually runs on (F7). It
becomes a **revenue line** only once there is a farm to host at.

## 4. Critic — where each path dies

**Path A dies on day one** because there is no money and no org to receive it.
It also dies in month 18 if the money does land, because the first grant
reviewer asks "how many people have you housed?" and the answer is zero (F4).

**Path B dies at the closing table.** Twenty-five percent down on a $250k
farmhouse is $62,500. If you had that, you would have said so. Seller financing
on a sober-living use is a long shot. And a $275k "fixer" farmhouse in Meshoppen
is a fixer: budget $40–80k before it passes a Ch. 717 inspection, and that
number is a guess until someone walks it.

**Path C dies three ways, and the first one is the real one:**

1. **No house manager.** This business is the house manager. If the person
   living there is not credible to residents, to probation officers, and to a
   discharge planner, the beds do not fill and the ones that do empty. You are
   two hours away running Nye AI. You cannot be the manager. A2 is the whole
   plan. *Kill condition: no credible candidate signed by day 45 — stop.*
2. **The township.** A landlord leases you a house, a neighbor sees six men come
   and go, the township cites the landlord for a "group home" in an R-1 zone,
   the landlord panics and terminates. The FHA (Cheltenham, Janush) is on your
   side in court; it is not on your side in a landlord's gut. *Mitigation:
   attorney-drafted lease clause plus the reasonable-accommodation letter in
   `03-zoning/` sent **before** the first resident moves in, not after the
   complaint.*
3. **One bad night.** An overdose on premises, a fight, a theft. In a
   licensed house with a manager, naloxone, and a written policy, it is a
   terrible night. In an unlicensed house with no manager, it is the end of
   the business and possibly a civil suit. *This is why the license is not
   optional even though F1 says it legally is.*

**What a resourced competitor does:** nothing. There is no competitive moat here
and there does not need to be — DDAP licenses ~400 houses statewide and they
are full. The risk is not competition; it is execution.

**Where the team was too comfortable:** every agent liked Path C because it is
cheap. Finance's revenue line assumes 6 residents paying $145/wk on time.
Sober-living collections are not clean. Assume **one resident in six is behind
at any time** — that is a 17% haircut on revenue, and it is in section 7.

## 5. Stress test — Path C only, since it is the survivor

| Scenario | What happens | Survivable? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Zero residents for 60 days** | Rent + utilities + manager stipend + insurance ≈ $2,900–$3,900/mo burned. Two months = $6–8k out of pocket. | Yes, inside the $8–14k start budget — barely. Month three with zero is the kill. |
| **House manager quits month 2** | House has no adult in charge. Residents drift. Township notices. | **Not survivable without a bench.** Line up a second candidate before opening. |
| **DDAP license takes 6 months** | You run private-pay only. No SCA referrals. Fill rate depends entirely on discharge planners and probation. | Survivable at 4–5 residents. This is the most likely scenario and the plan should assume it. |
| **Landlord terminates at 6 months** | 30-day scramble to re-house 6 men. | Survivable only with a 12-month lease and the FHA letter on file. Insist on both. |
| **You lose a week** | Nothing changes if the house manager is real. Everything changes if you are the de facto manager. | Depends entirely on A2. |
| **Pennycuick bill passes** | Licensure becomes mandatory. You already applied. | Neutral to positive — it shuts the unlicensed houses you compete with for referrals. |

## 6. The disagreement that stays in

**Sales** says a licensed house in a county with a 35/100k overdose rate fills
in 30 days from the SCA alone.

**Critic** says the SCA will not refer to a house that has been open six weeks
and is run by a web developer, and that the first 6 residents come from
private discharge planners and probation officers who will take a chance on a
clean house with a credible manager.

They are both describing the same first 90 days from different ends. **Plan on
the Critic's version.** If Sales turns out to be right, you fill faster and
nothing in the budget breaks.

**Market Research** and **Operations** disagree on location. Market says build
where the overdose burden is worst (Fayette, Susquehanna) because that is where
the referrals are. Operations says a house two and a half hours from the owner
with a single live-in manager is unsupervised, and that the leased-house MVP
should be in **Schuylkill or Berks** where you can be there in an hour. The
`02-land` recommendation of Susquehanna was written for buying and building.
**For leasing and operating, Operations wins:** stay within 90 minutes.

- **To run:** **5–8 hrs/week** if the house manager is real. **25–40 hrs/week**

### Implementation roadmap — to first revenue

**Week 1**
- Day 1: file the LLC. Day 1: call two land-use/nonprofit attorneys; book both.
- Day 2: call DDAP Licensing, (717) 783-8675 — fee, timeline, can a house with no residents apply.
- Day 3: post the house-manager role in two PA recovery groups; call two treatment-center discharge planners; ask each "if I had six licensed beds in 60 days, would you use them?"
- Day 4: three rental searches — Schuylkill, Berks, Wyoming — 4+ bedrooms, under $2,000.
- Day 5: one call to an insurance broker who writes recovery housing.

**Week 2**
- Attorney consult. Decide: LLC alone, or LLC + nonprofit shell for year-two grants. Get the lease clause and the FHA letter drafted.
- Tour three houses. Shortlist one.
- Two house-manager interviews minimum.

**Week 3**
- Sign a 12-month lease with the attorney's clause. Send the reasonable-accommodation letter to the township the same day.
- Submit the DDAP application.
- Sign the house manager.

**Weeks 4–6**
- Furnish to Ch. 717: beds, storage, detectors, lockable storage, kitchen.
- Write the house rules, intake packet, and relapse policy (NARR templates).
- Meet the SCA in person with the license application number in hand.
- Buy the Harvest Hosts membership. Book your first two farm stays for weekends in weeks 8–12.

**Weeks 7–9**
- Open private-pay. Target: first intake by **day 60**, three residents by day 75.
- **First revenue milestone: day 60–75.**

**Weeks 10–16**
- License lands (if A6 holds). Go back to the SCA with it.
- Target 6 residents by week 16. Breakeven.

**Month 6 checkpoint**
- Six residents, license in hand, manager stable → start the year-two file: Path B farmhouse, investor or seller-finance, nonprofit arm for grants, Harvest Hosts host application.
- Anything less → read the kill condition again.
