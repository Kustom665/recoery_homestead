# Cottage Materials — Design Spec, Sourcing, and Distribution

For the eight resident cottages in `04-architecture/unit-specs.md`. This is the
materials layer under that design: what to standardize, what each system costs
on verified 2026 numbers, who to call, and where ImportYeti helps and where it
does not.

**These cottages are the year-three build.** Nothing here gets bought before
the leased house is full and the farm is owned (`ceo-decision.md`). It is
written now so the numbers are locked and the quote calls are a list, not a
project.

---

## 1. The spec to standardize

Eight identical cottages, one plan review, one bulk order per material. Every
line below is chosen so a resident crew can build it under one supervisor and
a licensed trade only touches what the code requires.

| Element | Standard | Why this one |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Footprint | 20 × 20, 400 sq ft | Two ~90 sq ft sleeping rooms, shared living, half bath. § 717.29 wants 70 sq ft per single room; this clears it. |
| Foundation | Frost-protected shallow foundation (FPSF) slab, or concrete piers | Both code-approved in PA. FPSF is cheaper per sq ft; piers win on sloped or wet ground. Decide per site after the perc. |
| Shell | One of three: SIP kit, Amish-built delivered shell, or site-framed | Compared in section 3. |
| Roof | 6/12 gable, exposed-fastener metal, 26 ga SMP | 50-year material. Residents can install exposed-fastener; standing seam is a trade job. |
| Windows | Four 36 × 48 vinyl, new-construction fin, same SKU | One stock size for all eight houses means one bulk order and no custom lead time. |
| Doors | One 36" exterior steel, two 30" interior hollow-core | Stock. |
| Heat and cooling | One 9,000–12,000 BTU mini-split per cottage, same model | 400 sq ft well-insulated needs 9k; 12k is the safer buy for a drafty first winter. |
| Plumbing | Half bath only: toilet, lavatory, PEX. No shower. | Showers live in the bath house. This is the single biggest cost cut in the design. |
| Electrical | 60 A subpanel, 6 circuits | Licensed electrician. Non-negotiable. |
| Interior | Drywall, paint, LVP floor, vaulted ceiling | Resident labor. |

## 2. Bill of materials — verified lines first

Prices are October 2026 listings or guides, cited in `sources.md`. "Verified"
means a current price was found; "quote" means it was not and the line is
carried at the `unit-specs.md` planning figure until a vendor prices it. **Do
not add the column up into a total yet** — three systems are still quotes.

### Foundation — verified range

| Option | Cost, 400 sq ft | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FPSF slab | **$3,200–$4,800** | $8–12/sq ft guide range. Penn State PHRC calls FPSF code-approved in PA but niche; under-slab insulation is capped near R-10 for a heated building. |
| Concrete piers | **$4,800–$15,000** | 8–12" piers at 6–8 ft spacing, below frost line. The high end is deep frost plus rock. |

Frost depth drives both. Get the county figure from the code office before
choosing.

### Shell — three real options

**20 × 20 with 8-ft walls and a 6/12 gable = ~640 sq ft of wall and ~450 sq ft
of roof, about 1,090 sq ft of envelope.**

| Option | Material cost | Labor | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A — SIP kit** | **$7,600–$13,100** panels + **$550–$4,400** delivery | Resident crew sets panels in days; needs a forklift or small crane on site | $7–12/sq ft panel range, national average $9.70. Delivery $0.50–4/sq ft, high end for rural drops. |
| **B — Amish-built shell, delivered** | **Quote** — not priced online for this size | None on site; it arrives built | The cheapest route for a 20 × 20 in Pennsylvania if the quotes come in where the trade usually does. Erie County's Amish Meadow Lark publishes $1,120–$5,120 for shed-class 8 × 8 to 12 × 32, $3 per loaded mile outside Erie/Crawford, $350 escort fee over 14 ft wide. A true 20 × 20 cabin shell needs three quotes from Lancaster, Snyder, or Mifflin County builders. |
| **C — Site-framed by residents** | **Quote** — lumber-yard takeoff | Highest labor, highest teaching value | The Recovery Boys model. Price it from a local yard's takeoff on the standard plan; do not guess it. |

**Named SIP makers to call, both in Pennsylvania:**

- **The Murus Company**, Mansfield, PA — 800-626-8787. Tioga County, roughly an
  hour from the Bradford County parcels in `land-candidates.md`. Short haul
  means the delivery line sits at the bottom of its range.
- **Future Building of America**, Farrell, PA — (724) 342-9750. OSB-skinned
  foam-core panels, R-values to R-38. Western PA; longer haul to the northern
  tier.

Premier SIPs, Enercept, Insulspan, Thermocore, and Extreme Panel are the
national names; none was verified this round. Call the two PA makers first.

## 3. Where ImportYeti helps — and the honest limit

**What it is.** ImportYeti indexes U.S. Customs bills of lading — public under
the 1789 Tariff Act — and shows, for any U.S. importer, which overseas
factories ship to it and how often; and for any factory, every U.S. company it
ships to. Company-level profiles and shipment counts are free. Per-shipment
detail and data export sit behind a paid tier. It covers U.S. and Mexico
imports only.

**I could not reach it from this environment** — both importyeti.com and its
docs returned a Cloudflare 403 — so the workflow below is for you to run
in a browser. It takes about twenty minutes per product category.

**The workflow:**

1. **Search the product, not the brand.** Type `mini split` (or `ductless heat
   pump`). The results list U.S. importers ranked by shipment count. The top of
   that list is the distributors who move the most units.
2. **Open the importer you already buy from.** Search `Pioneer` or `Senville`
   by company name. Their profile shows the overseas factories shipping to them
   and how many containers a year. That factory name is the OEM.
3. **Open the factory.** Its profile lists every U.S. company it ships to. If a
   Gree or Midea plant ships to five brands you recognize, it is vetted. Look
   for a **smaller U.S. importer on that same list** — one that buys the same
   unit and sells with a U.S. warranty at a thinner margin than the brand.
4. **Repeat for windows and roofing coil.** Vinyl windows: search `vinyl
   window`. Steel roofing: search `galvalume coil` — the roll-formers you buy
   panels from import the coil.

**Where it will not help, and why:**

| Material | ImportYeti value | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mini-splits | **High** | Nearly all are built by Gree, Midea, or AUX and rebadged. The factory is the product. |
| Vinyl windows | Medium | Some imported, much domestic. Worth one search. |
| Metal roofing | Low–medium | Panels are roll-formed here from coil that may be imported. Buy from the local roll-former. |
| SIPs | **None** | Domestic. Murus is an hour away. |
| Amish shells | **None** | Local by definition. |
| Lumber, drywall, concrete | None | Commodity, domestic, buy local. |

**The trap.** Buying direct from the factory makes you the importer of record:
a customs broker, duties, a minimum order that is usually a container, no U.S.
warranty, and no one to call when a compressor fails in February. **Eight
mini-splits is not a container.** The realistic ImportYeti play is step 3 —
find the smaller U.S. distributor already importing the identical unit — not
importing it yourself. The second realistic play is leverage: knowing that
Pioneer and Senville share a factory is a negotiating fact when you ask a
distributor for an eight-unit price.

## 4. Distribution — getting it to a rural site

| Material | How it arrives | What to plan for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SIP kit | Flatbed, one or two loads for eight cottages | Forklift or small crane on site the day of delivery. Rural drop adds $0.50–4/sq ft. Murus from Mansfield to Bradford County is a short haul. |
| Amish shell | Built elsewhere, hauled on a trailer, set on the foundation | Wide-load permit and escort ($350 per the Erie example) over 14 ft. Access road must take a 40-ft rig. Gravel drive and turnaround before the first delivery. |
| Metal roofing | Local roll-former cuts to length, delivers bundled | Order all eight roofs at once; cut lengths are custom and free. |
| Windows, doors, mini-splits | Big-box or distributor, pallet | Stage in the barn. Order the 32 windows as one PO. |
| Lumber, drywall | Local yard, boom truck | Yard delivers to a driveway, not a field. The farm road gets built before the lumber comes. |

**Sequence the deliveries after the driveway and the barn.** Every material
above wants a dry, flat place to sit and a road a truck can turn on. That is
Phase 0 and Phase 3 in `04-architecture/site-plan.md`, and it is why the barn
comes before the cottages.

## 5. The calls

1. **Murus**, 800-626-8787 — a budget number for eight 20 × 20 SIP packages
   delivered to Bradford or Wyoming County.
2. **Three Amish cabin builders**, Lancaster / Snyder / Mifflin County — a
   delivered 20 × 20 shell, 8-ft walls, 6/12 gable, no interior. Ask the escort
   and mileage fee.
3. **One local metal roll-former** — 26 ga SMP exposed-fastener, ~450 sq ft
   cut to length, times eight.
4. **One electrician and one plumber** — per-cottage price for eight identical
   rough-ins. Identical is the word that gets the price down.
5. **ImportYeti, in a browser** — `mini split`, then `Pioneer`, then the
   factory. Twenty minutes. Write down the smaller importers.
6. **The county code office** — frost depth, and whether FPSF has been approved
   there before. If it has not, piers.

None of these is a purchase. All of them turn a quote line into a number.
