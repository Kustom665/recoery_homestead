# The Odds and Ends

Development package for a working-farm recovery community in Pennsylvania.

A compound of tiny homes on 20–40 rural acres where men in long-term recovery
live, work the land, and build the place themselves. Modeled on **Jacob's Ladder
at Brookside Farm** — the six-month, farm-based program in the 2018 documentary
*Recovery Boys*.

This repo covers land, funding, architecture, permits, and licensure.

## The four things you should know before reading anything else

**1. Lancaster doesn't work.** ~$22,000/acre limestone farmland, ~125,000 acres
locked under permanent preservation easements, 99% of southeastern PA zoned, and
limestone geology that disqualifies you from the sewage-planning exemption. Land
cost alone eats the budget. → [`02-land/lancaster-reality-check.md`](02-land/lancaster-reality-check.md)

**2. 822 PA municipalities have no zoning ordinance at all.** In rural counties,
only 34% have zoning. No ordinance means no variance, no hearing, no neighbor
petition. This is the highest-leverage decision in the project.
→ [`03-zoning/zoning-strategy.md`](03-zoning/zoning-strategy.md)

**3. Campers can't be permanent housing in PA** — except in a properly zoned RV
or mobile home park. There are four legal paths around this and one of them is
clean. → [`04-architecture/camper-phase.md`](04-architecture/camper-phase.md)

**4. Build a cottage cluster, not a bunkhouse.** One large structure housing many
unrelated residents can get classified R-2 or I-1 institutional, triggering
sprinklers, commercial egress, and full ADA. That's a six-figure surprise. Small
detached cottages stay R-3. → [`04-architecture/site-plan.md`](04-architecture/site-plan.md)

## The numbers

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Land | 20–40 acres, rural PA |
| Residents at full build-out | 16–24 |
| Program length | 6 months + transition tier |
| Capital cost (Phases 0–3, w/ contingency) | **~$1.29M – $2.90M** |
| Annual operating at 16 residents | **~$299K – $507K** |
| Cost per resident per day | **~$51 – $87** |

That last figure is the one that wins grant applications. It compares very
favorably to inpatient treatment or incarceration — get your county's real
numbers for the contrast.

## Do these three things this week

1. **Call two PA land-use attorneys** and book consultations. Most important
   hire in the project.
2. **Call the Single County Authority** in Schuylkill or Fayette. Ask what they
   are short of. Referrals decide whether your beds fill.
3. **Download 28 Pa. Code Chapter 717** and read it. Its physical-plant
   requirements are architectural constraints — design backward from them.

All three are free. All three change what you build.

## Caveats

Every figure here is a **modeled planning range, not a quote.** They exist to
size a grant request and tell you what you're walking into. Replace each one
with a real bid before committing.

Program deadlines and dollar amounts change — verify current windows directly
with each funder.

**This is not legal advice.** Two professionals are non-negotiable and both pay
for themselves several times over on a project like this:

- A **Pennsylvania land-use attorney** — zoning, easements, entity structure,
  and the resident-labor question
- A **PA-licensed architect or engineer** — stamped drawings

Budget for both in Phase 0.
